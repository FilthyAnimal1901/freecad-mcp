## TITLE OF THE INVENTION

High-Pressure Acoustic Cavitation Vessel for Density-Differential and Tensile-Stress Mineral Separation

---

## TECHNICAL FIELD

1. The present invention relates to the field of hydrometallurgy and mineral processing, and more particularly to an apparatus and method utilizing hydro-acoustic resonance and localized pressure differentials to achieve mineral liberation from ore matrices without chemical lixiviants. The invention combines controlled hydrodynamic cavitation with acoustic radiation pressure to simultaneously fracture mineral bonds via tensile stress and stratify liberated particles by density differential.

---

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

2. Conventional mineral processing relies on mechanical comminution (crushing and grinding) to liberate valuable minerals from gangue. These methods apply compressive stress to fracture ore, requiring substantial energy input — typically 20–30 kWh per tonne of ore processed. The resulting particle size distributions are often broader than desired, leading to over-grinding of softer minerals and under-liberation of harder inclusions.

3. Chemical lixiviation using cyanide, acids, or other reagents presents significant environmental and regulatory challenges. The mining industry requires alternative liberation methods that are energy-efficient, environmentally benign, and capable of selective liberation based on the differential mechanical properties of mineral phases.

4. Prior art in acoustic mineral processing (e.g., ultrasonic cleaning, sonochemical leaching) has been limited to batch-scale laboratory applications. These systems lack the pressure containment, flow geometry, and frequency tuning required for continuous industrial-scale operation at the throughput rates demanded by commercial mining operations.

5. There remains a need for a continuous-flow mineral processing apparatus that achieves selective mineral liberation by exploiting the differential tensile strength and density properties of mineral phases through controlled acoustic energy input, without chemical reagents, and at industrially relevant throughput rates.

---

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

6. The present invention provides an apparatus for mineral separation comprising a monolithic forged pressure vessel defining a reaction chamber, an inlet for receiving a mineral-bearing slurry, one or more acoustic generators coupled to the vessel and configured to induce hydrodynamic cavitation within the slurry, and a discharge outlet. The vessel geometry is configured to produce a fluid singularity zone where localized tensile stress generated by cavitation bubble collapse exceeds the bond energy of the target mineral matrix.

7. In a preferred embodiment, the apparatus operates at dual frequencies: a primary frequency in the range of 250–400 Hz selected to match an axial resonant mode of the vessel's internal fluid column, producing bulk cavitation and macro-scale flow instabilities; and a secondary frequency in the range of 35–45 kHz, selected to achieve half-wavelength acoustic transparency through the vessel wall, thereby transmitting acoustic energy from externally-mounted transducers directly into the slurry with minimal reflection loss.

8. The apparatus further comprises an annular overflow weir at the vessel's upper periphery, functioning as a surface overflow rate (SOR) collection system for density-stratified slurry. Lighter gangue particles, displaced to the fluid surface by acoustic radiation pressure, are continuously decanted over the weir, while denser valuable minerals (gold, platinum group metals, heavy mineral sands) settle toward the vessel's conical discharge.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Vessel Construction

9. The vessel is manufactured as a single, seamless, hot-forged axisymmetric solid from SA-508 Grade 3 Class 1 low-alloy steel, or equivalent material meeting ASME Section III requirements. The monolithic construction eliminates welded joints, achieving a stress concentration factor (SCF) approaching unity (1.0) at all structural transitions, thereby ensuring infinite fatigue life under dynamic acoustic loading.

10. The vessel comprises the following integral structural elements:

   (a) **Main Cylindrical Chamber:** outer diameter 1850 mm, inner diameter 1700 mm, providing a uniform wall thickness of 75 mm throughout the straight section. The straight section height is 2800 mm.

   (b) **Bottom Conical Taper:** a 15-degree half-angle conical section transitioning from the 1700 mm main inner diameter to a 678 mm exit bore. The taper maintains the uniform 75 mm wall thickness. The taper height is approximately 1907 mm as determined by the relationship h = (R_main - R_exit) / tan(alpha), where alpha is the half-angle.

   (c) **Top Flange:** an integral flange 40 mm thick extending radially outward from the top of the straight section to a radius of 1010 mm, providing a sealing surface for the vessel closure.

   (d) **Bottom Flange:** an integral flange 30 mm thick at the exit bore, extending to a radius of 500 mm, providing connection to downstream processing equipment.

   (e) All transitions between structural elements incorporate forged fillet radii of 40–60 mm to eliminate stress concentrations.

### 2. Acoustic System

11. **The Critical Dimension — Wall Thickness as Acoustic Filter:**

The 75 mm wall thickness is not arbitrary. It is selected to be exactly one half-wavelength (lambda/2) of longitudinal sound waves in SA-508 steel at a frequency of approximately 39,733 Hz (39.73 kHz), as determined by the relationship:

```
f_transparency = c_steel / (2 * t_wall)
                = 5960 m/s / (2 * 0.075 m)
                = 39,733 Hz
```

At this frequency, the vessel wall exhibits acoustic transparency — the impedance mismatch between the steel wall (Z_steel = 46.79 MRayl) and the mineral slurry (Z_slurry = 2.20 MRayl) is effectively cancelled by destructive interference of reflected waves within the wall. The power transmission coefficient at the half-wavelength condition approaches unity, compared with only 17.2% at non-resonant frequencies.

12. This enables externally-mounted piezoelectric or magnetostrictive transducers to inject acoustic energy through the pressure-containing wall without penetrating it, maintaining vessel integrity at the rated design pressure of 172.4 MPa.

13. **Dual-Frequency Operation:**

The acoustic system operates simultaneously at two distinct frequency regimes:

   (a) **Primary Frequency (250–400 Hz):** An acoustic actuator housed in the resonator collar on the exterior of the bottom conical taper generates acoustic waves at approximately 333 Hz. This frequency is selected to be proximate to the first axial resonant mode of the fluid column within the straight section (f_1 = c_slurry / (2*L) = 1520 / (2*2.8) = 271.4 Hz). The slight detuning from the fundamental creates a beating pattern that sweeps the cavitation zone across the vessel cross-section, preventing localized erosion.

   (b) **Secondary Frequency (35–45 kHz):** An array of piezoelectric transducers mounted on the exterior of the straight section, operating at the wall transparency frequency of 39.73 kHz. This frequency produces microcavitation with bubble collapse pressures in the gigapascal range (estimated 8.92 GPa at 0.50 MPa acoustic pressure amplitude), sufficient to fracture all known mineral matrices.

### 3. Mechanisms of Mineral Liberation

14. **Tensile-Stress Liberation via Cavitation Bubble Collapse:**

When a cavitation bubble collapses near a mineral particle surface, the asymmetric collapse generates a micro-jet directed toward the particle with velocity exceeding 1000 m/s. The resulting localized pressure at the impact point is described by the water-hammer equation:

```
P_collapse = rho_slurry * c_slurry * v_jet
```

The bubble dynamics are governed by the Rayleigh-Plesset equation:

```
R*R'' + (3/2)*R'^2 = (1/rho) * [P_B(t) - P_inf(t)] - 4*nu*R'/R - 2*gamma/(rho*R)
```

where R is the instantaneous bubble radius, P_B is the internal bubble pressure, P_inf is the far-field pressure including the acoustic driving term, nu is the kinematic viscosity, and gamma is the surface tension.

15. Numerical solution of the Rayleigh-Plesset equation for the vessel operating conditions yields the following collapse pressures:

| Driving Frequency | Compression Ratio (R0/R_min) | Max Collapse Pressure | Exceeds Quartz (48 MPa) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 333 Hz | 11.6:1 | 0.91 GPa | Yes (19x) |
| 271 Hz (n=1 axial) | 10.8:1 | 1.61 GPa | Yes (34x) |
| 39.73 kHz (wall transparency) | 43.1:1 | 8.92 GPa | Yes (186x) |

16. These collapse pressures exceed the tensile strength of all common gangue minerals:

| Mineral | Tensile Strength | Exceeded by 333 Hz | Exceeded by 39.73 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quartz | 48 MPa | 19x | 186x |
| Calcite | 12 MPa | 76x | 743x |
| Pyrite | 25 MPa | 36x | 357x |
| Feldspar | 10 MPa | 91x | 892x |

17. **Density-Differential Stratification via Acoustic Radiation Force:**

Simultaneously with cavitation-induced liberation, the standing acoustic wave field within the vessel exerts a Langevin acoustic radiation force on suspended particles. This force is a function of the density contrast between the particle and the carrier fluid:

```
F_rad = -(pi * P_0^2 * V_p * beta_f) / (2 * lambda) * phi(beta, rho)
```

where phi is the acoustic contrast factor:

```
phi = (5*rho_p - 2*rho_f) / (2*rho_p + rho_f) - beta_p/beta_f
```

Particles with positive acoustic contrast (dense minerals: gold rho=19300, PGMs rho=12000–22500) migrate toward pressure nodes, while particles with lower density contrast (gangue: quartz rho=2650, calcite rho=2710) migrate toward pressure antinodes.

18. The vessel geometry is configured such that the pressure antinodes of the axial standing wave field are positioned near the fluid surface at the vessel's upper rim, where the annular overflow weir collects the gangue-enriched surface layer.

### 4. Overflow Collection System (Charcoal Infinity Lip)

19. The upper rim of the vessel incorporates an integral annular overflow weir, referred to herein as the "Infinity Lip." This is a continuous, open-topped trough extending around the full outer perimeter of the top flange. The trough comprises:

   (a) An outer wall extending 60 mm above the top flange surface.
   (b) A channel 25 mm in radial width.
   (c) A trough depth of 45 mm below the outer wall top edge.
   (d) A base thickness of 15 mm.

20. The Infinity Lip functions as a surface overflow rate (SOR) collector. As density-stratified slurry rises within the vessel, the lighter gangue-enriched fraction spills over the inner edge of the trough and is collected in the annular channel for separate discharge. The surface overflow rate is controlled by the vessel's feed rate and the acoustic radiation pressure, which modulates the effective density profile of the fluid column.

### 5. Resonator Housing (Bottom Resonator Collar)

21. The exterior of the bottom conical taper incorporates an integral reinforced collar positioned approximately 200 mm above the bottom flange. The collar is 300 mm in axial height and extends 40 mm radially beyond the nominal outer taper surface.

22. The collar provides a structurally rigid mounting platform for the primary acoustic actuator (magnetostrictive or electromagnetic) operating at 333 Hz. The increased wall thickness in this region (effectively 115 mm: 75 mm vessel wall + 40 mm collar) provides acoustic impedance matching between the actuator and the vessel wall at the lower frequency, and resists the cyclic mechanical loading imposed by the actuator.

### 6. Pressure Control System

23. The apparatus includes a feedback control system maintaining the cavitation number (sigma) within the range of 0.05 to 0.15 during operation. The cavitation number is defined as:

```
sigma = (P_static - P_vapor) / (0.5 * rho * v^2)
```

24. Real-time pressure transducers mounted at the vessel inlet, the midpoint of the straight section, and the exit bore provide continuous measurement of the static pressure field. A programmable logic controller adjusts the slurry feed pump speed and the acoustic actuator power to maintain sigma within the specified range, corresponding to flow velocities in the range of 135–235 m/s and Reynolds numbers exceeding 2.0 x 10^8, ensuring fully turbulent flow conducive to cavitation inception.

## ABSTRACT

A monolithic forged pressure vessel for mineral separation utilizes dual-frequency acoustic cavitation to achieve selective mineral liberation and density-differential stratification without chemical reagents. The vessel wall thickness is selected to equal one half-wavelength of longitudinal sound in the wall material at a predetermined ultrasonic frequency, rendering the wall acoustically transparent and enabling externally-mounted transducers to inject acoustic energy through the intact pressure boundary. A lower-frequency acoustic actuator housed in an integral resonator collar induces bulk hydrodynamic cavitation, producing localized tensile stresses exceeding the bond energy of mineral matrices. An integral annular overflow weir at the vessel rim collects density-stratified gangue displaced by acoustic radiation pressure, while concentrated valuable minerals discharge through a conical exit bore.
